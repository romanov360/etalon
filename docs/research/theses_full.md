
### 4.7/10 — Interferon (CPO digital twin + test/yield data layer)

**one_liner**: Cloud software that predicts, tests, and certifies co-packaged optics — link-level E-O-E digital twins for CPO architects plus known-good-optical-engine analytics for the test floor.

**wedge**: Two-sided entry, one data model. Side A: a statistically-aware (Monte Carlo corner) link simulator spanning SerDes→optical engine→fiber plant→receiver with BER/power/thermal prediction, sold via API to CPO system architecture teams at switch/XPU vendors and hyperscalers. Side B: test-content and analytics software sitting on top of the probe hardware incumbents (Teradyne/ficonTEC, FormFactor TRITON, Aehr burn-in) — test-time compression, wafer-measurement-to-packaged-performance correlation models, and independent known-good-die binning/certification for optical engines. First customer profile: a hyperscaler or Broadcom-ecosystem OSAT drowning in the fact that one bad engine among 24+ scraps an expensive CPO assembly.

**why_now**: Testing is now the loudest bottleneck in CPO mass production (TrendForce): >100s per PIC, no unified standard, manual optical probing, TSMC COUPE ramping 2H 2026. Meta disclosed a 90-million-hour CPO reliability validation — the qualification burden is enormous and hyperscalers will pay to compress it. Keysight paid twice in 12 months (OSG + VPIphotonics) for exactly the link-simulation capability, proving budget exists, but its stack is desktop-era and siloed. Semitronix bought Luceda specifically to fuse design with wafer-test/yield data — validating the loop — yet no independent Western vendor offers it. 2026-2027 first-deployment failure data is about to exist and someone will own it.

**market**: Wafer-level silicon photonic test systems: ~$670M (2024) → $1.6B (2031, 13.6% CAGR) — but that's hardware; the software/analytics layer on top is greenfield, realistically a 10-20% attach ($100-300M SAM near-term) plus per-wafer-priced test-time-reduction economics that scale with CPO volume (port shipments 50K in 2023 → 4.5M forecast 2027). The digital-twin side sells to perhaps 20 organizations on earth, but at hyperscaler contract sizes ($1-10M/year each). Combined credible ARR path: $20-50M by 2029, with strategic value to Teradyne/Keysight/Advantest far exceeding revenue multiples — Teradyne already bought Quantifi for less capability.

**competition**: Keysight (VPI Optical Link in ADS — the incumbent link simulator; strong but not cloud-native, not yield-statistical, and conflicted as a test-hardware vendor); Synopsys/Ansys (device-level, weak at system); Teradyne/Quantifi and FormFactor/Advantest/TEL (own the probe hardware and will resist an independent analytics layer — or acquire it); Semitronix/Luceda (the same thesis, but Chinese-owned and thus excluded from US hyperscaler/defense test floors); in-house hyperscaler tools (Meta/Google will build some of this themselves — your counter is cross-vendor breadth).

Standards participation (OIF, OCP, the missing KGD standard) can institutionalize your formats.

### 4.3/10 — Aperture (neutral, AI-native photonic EDA)

**one_liner**: The independent, cloud-native, agentic design platform for photonic ICs — the 'Figma vs Adobe' play against the Synopsys/Keysight/Semitronix roll-ups, built open-core on gdsfactory.

### 4.3/10 — TrueNorth Photonics (defense PNT silicon photonics, merchant model)

**one_liner**: Fabless silicon-photonic inertial navigation and timing engines — the second source to Anello and the merchant supplier the FMCW/PNT wave lacks — sold to defense integrators who pay now.

**wedge**: A navigation-grade silicon-photonic optical gyroscope + IMU module, fabbed on open foundry platforms (Tower PH18/SiN, Ligentec low-loss SiN at <0.5dB/m), sold as a component/module to autonomy primes and integrators (USV/AUV/drone makers like the HavocAI/BlackSea/Vatn cohort Anello already serves, plus European equivalents). Deliberately merchant: Anello, Aeva, SiLC, Voyant are all vertically integrated; Tier-1s and primes have no one to design in.

**why_now**: GPS jamming went from edge case to daily reality (Ukraine, Baltic, Middle East), and defense procurement has caught up: Anello's $20M APFIT production award (Jan 2026) proves DoD is buying photonic gyros in production quantity, not SBIR quantity; its oversubscribed May 2026 round with Lockheed Martin Ventures proves the cap-table appetite. Single-vendor risk is already a procurement concern: Anello is effectively alone, and defense buyers structurally demand second sources.

**market**: Honest sizing: high-performance inertial (FOG/RLG replacement) is ~$1.5-2.5B/year, with the GPS-denied navigation segment growing 15-20% as jamming spreads; chip-scale PNT modules could take $300-600M of it by 2030. **competition**: Anello (the category leader, 5+ years ahead on productization and DoD relationships — you are explicitly the #2); incumbent FOG/RLG vendors (Honeywell, KVH, Exail — better performance today, 10x the size/power/cost, and they are your acquirers more than competitors); MEMS IMU vendors from below (fine for low grade, can't reach navigation grade); Aeva's defense wing and orphaned FMCW assets (Scantinel→MicroVision) on the LiDAR side. ### 4/10 — Kelvin Light (athermal high-efficiency multi-wavelength laser source)

### 3.7/10 — OpenBeam (merchant OCI-MSA optical engine, non-TSMC)

**one_liner**: The multi-source UCIe-fronted optical engine the hyperscalers just standardized for — built on GlobalFoundries/Tower instead of TSMC, sold to everyone who isn't NVIDIA.

**wedge**: An OCI-MSA-compliant optical chiplet (UCIe electrical side, parallel CWDM WDM optical side, targeting the standard's path to 3.2Tb/s per link) fabbed on GF Fotonix (monolithic CMOS+photonics, proven with Lightmatter/Ayar) or Tower/OpenLight (integrated lasers), sold to custom-XPU programs — hyperscaler ASIC teams (Google, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft via Alchip/Broadcom-custom/Marvell-rivals) and AMD-ecosystem players who need optical scale-up but refuse single-vendor lock-in on Ayar Labs or a Marvell subsidiary.

**why_now**: The OCI MSA (March 2026, founded by AMD, Broadcom, Meta, Microsoft, NVIDIA, OpenAI) explicitly standardizes a multi-vendor optical scale-up interface — hyperscalers created it precisely because they want second and third sources. Consolidation just deleted the independents: Celestial→Marvell, Nubis→Ciena, DustPhotonics→Credo, Enosemi→AMD. Ayar Labs is the only scaled independent chiplet vendor left, and it just joined NVIDIA's NVLink Fusion orbit — making a neutral alternative more valuable, not less. GF is hungry for anchor photonics customers post-AMF acquisition and will co-invest in enablement.

**market**: Optical scale-up is the genuinely enormous prize: CPO $8-10B by 2030, with the scale-up tier (GPU-to-GPU/memory) called 'a new market' by LightCounting and validated by Celestial's $3.25B exit and Marvell's claimed ~$10B addressable. A merchant engine vendor winning 2-3 hyperscaler custom-silicon sockets ships millions of chiplets: $500M-1B+ revenue potential by 2030-2032. This is the biggest TAM of any thesis here — and the hardest to reach.

**competition**: Savage, and mostly better-funded: Ayar Labs ($870M raised, NVIDIA/AMD/Intel all invested, NVLink Fusion member, 5-year head start); Marvell/Celestial (owns the AWS-adjacent custom-XPU channel); Broadcom (will offer OCI engines alongside its custom-ASIC business); Lightmatter (interposer approach, ~$12.5B IPO war chest); Intel's OCI chiplet program; plus TSMC COUPE gravitationally pulling the biggest customers to its ecosystem. You win only on the specific vector of neutrality + non-TSMC supply + OCI-native design — and only if hyperscalers' multi-sourcing rhetoric converts to purchase orders for a startup.

**moat**: Thin until a design win, then extremely thick: a socket in a hyperscaler XPU program is a 3-5 year committed ramp with co-developed test/packaging flows. Pre-win, the defensible assets are microring/WDM design IP that hits OCI specs on GF's process (nontrivial porting work others haven't done), laser-integration route via OpenLight/Tower that TSMC-ecosystem rivals lack, and the neutrality position itself.

**capital_intensity**: High — this is honest deep-tech fabless, not lean: $30-50M to a credible sampling chiplet (multiple full mask sets at >$500K each, 4-6 month cycles, packaging/test development, a 40+ person mixed photonics/SerDes team), $150-300M cumulative to production qualification. The Ayar fundraising history ($870M) is the realistic benchmark. Requires a strategic CVC (AMD, a hyperscaler, GF itself) by Series A or don't start.

**software_mvp**: This week: an OCI-MSA link-compliance and architecture-exploration toolkit — open-source models of the MSA's UCIe+WDM stack, letting XPU architects sweep engine configurations (wavelength counts, launch power vs the ~24.5dBm budget, ring tuning overhead at ~0.12nm/mW) against their bandwidth/pJ-per-bit targets. It builds relationships with the exact 10 architecture teams who will someday buy the chiplet, shapes the spec conversation, and costs nothing but engineering time.
